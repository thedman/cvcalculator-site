What happens to your defined benefit pension when you leave your employer in Canada?

Whether you're resigning, being laid off, or considering retirement, your accrued DB pension does not automatically disappear. Depending on your plan and the law that governs it, you may retain a deferred pension or be eligible to transfer its commuted value. Before your options statement arrives, use our commuted value of pension calculator to estimate the value and explore the possible LIRA and taxable portions.

By the CVCalculator Team · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

This guide covers defined benefit (DB) pension plans only. A defined benefit plan promises retirement income under a plan formula; the plan sponsor bears the investment risk. If you have a defined contribution (DC) plan, your benefit is based on an account balance rather than the commuted value of a DB pension. Your transfer options still depend on the applicable pension law and plan. Check your pension statement or ask the administrator if you are unsure which type you have.

Your pension doesn't disappear when you leave

Leaving your employer does not automatically erase the DB pension you have earned. Vesting describes your entitlement to that benefit under the law governing the plan. The rules are not the same across Canada, and leaving a job does not always end plan membership at the same time.

For Ontario-regulated plans, members whose employment ended on or after July 1, 2012 generally have immediate vesting. Multi-employer and jointly sponsored plans can apply specific rules about when membership ends. Check which law and plan rules apply to you; do not assume a two-year wait.

What you can do with your accrued pension depends on the plan and your circumstances. A deferred pension and, where available, a commuted value transfer are two common paths.

For OPSEU Pension Plan members, the OPTrust guide to leaving employment explains how deferred entitlement, unreduced-pension eligibility and special employment circumstances affect the available choices.

Your two main options at termination

01

Deferred pension — stay in the plan

Your accrued pension remains in the plan and becomes payable under its retirement rules. Its amount reflects the plan formula and your accrued service when membership ends; future adjustments, including indexation or other plan features, vary by plan.

02

Commuted value — take the lump sum

If you are eligible, your pension can be converted to a present-value lump sum called the commuted value, calculated under applicable actuarial standards such as CIA §3500. A transfer may go to a locked-in arrangement such as a LIRA or, if accepted, another pension plan. For transfers to a LIRA, Income Tax Regulations section 8517 generally limits the tax-deferred amount; an excess may be paid as taxable cash. See how the taxable portion is treated.

Important: There is no universal Canadian election window. Your options statement should tell you which benefits and transfer choices are available and the deadline for responding. That deadline can depend on the governing law and your plan. Read the statement promptly and ask the administrator if anything is unclear.

What is vesting and are you vested?

Vesting is your legal entitlement to the pension benefit earned under a plan. The date and consequences of vesting depend on the law governing that plan. In Ontario, immediate vesting applies to members who terminated employment on or after July 1, 2012; for some multi-employer or jointly sponsored plans, termination of plan membership rather than a change of employer determines when the member is treated as having terminated employment.

British Columbia also provides immediate vesting under its pension standards. Do not assume that a rule in one province applies to your plan, or that leaving an employer automatically ends membership in a multi-employer plan.

If your benefit is not vested under the rules that apply to you, the plan administrator can explain whether a contribution refund or another benefit is payable. Do not assume that a refund equals only your own contributions in every plan.

What happens to the commuted value after transfer

When an eligible member transfers a commuted value to a LIRA, Income Tax Regulations section 8517 generally limits how much can be transferred tax-deferred. Depending on the plan and applicable rules, the result may include:

  • Locked-in transfer — an eligible amount may move to a LIRA or another permitted locked-in arrangement. A receiving pension plan may also be an option if it accepts the transfer. The funds are generally reserved for retirement income; unlocking exceptions depend on the jurisdiction and account terms.
  • Taxable excess — an amount above the applicable tax-deferred transfer limit may be paid in cash, subject to withholding, and included in your taxable income. If you have sufficient RRSP room, a separate RRSP contribution may affect your tax position; seek individual tax advice.

The termination timeline

Step 1
Termination date
Your employment ends. Plan membership may end then or later, depending on the plan; your administrator determines your accrued benefit and available options.
Step 2
Termination package received
The plan administrator provides an options statement under the rules that govern your plan. If a commuted value transfer is available, the statement should set out the official value, choices and applicable deadlines.
Step 3
Election window
Check the deadline stated in your options statement; it is not the same as the administrator's deadline to send that statement. Review the benefits, tax implications and any transfer restrictions before deciding.
Step 4
Transfer or deferral
An eligible transfer is processed to the permitted destination, with any taxable excess handled under the applicable rules. If you retain a deferred pension, it remains payable under the plan's terms.

Jurisdictional differences to be aware of

The law governing your pension may be provincial or federal. Termination options, plan membership, transfer eligibility and locked-in requirements differ. For example:

  • Ontario — the Pension Benefits Act provides immediate vesting for applicable members whose employment ended on or after July 1, 2012. Leaving an employer in a multi-employer or jointly sponsored plan may not end plan membership. Transfer eligibility and locked-in options require a plan-specific check. Ontario plans include OMERS and HOOPP.
  • British Columbia — its Pension Benefits Standards Act provides immediate vesting; transfer and unlocking rules depend on that legislation and the plan.
  • Quebec — supplemental pension plans are governed by Quebec law, with their own membership, transfer and locked-in rules. Ask the administrator which provisions apply.
  • Federal plans — the federal public service pension plan is governed by the Public Service Superannuation Act, not the Pension Benefits Standards Act, 1985. Other federal public-sector plans have their own statutes; federally regulated private-sector plans may fall under the Pension Benefits Standards Act, 1985.

Always verify the specific law and plan rules that apply before making an election.

Should you take the commuted value or defer?

This is the central question — and there's no universal right answer. It depends on your age, health, investment discipline, the plan's indexation provisions, your need for guaranteed income, and many other factors. Frequently asked questions

What happens to my defined benefit pension when I quit my job in Canada?

Your accrued DB pension does not automatically disappear. Depending on the law governing your plan and its terms, you may retain a deferred pension or be eligible to transfer its commuted value. Leaving an employer does not always end plan membership, and a transfer is not always available.

How long do I have to decide what to do with my pension?

There is no single Canadian election deadline. Your options statement should set out the choices available and when you must act. Do not confuse your response deadline with the administrator's deadline for issuing that statement; ask the administrator if the timing is unclear.

Do I lose my DB pension if I leave before retirement?

Leaving before retirement does not automatically erase a DB pension. Your entitlement depends on the law governing your plan, when plan membership ends and the benefit you have earned. If a benefit is not vested, ask the administrator what refund or other payment, if any, is available.

How do I estimate my commuted value before my package arrives?

Use a Canadian commuted value calculator with your accrued annual pension, date of birth, termination date, EURD, survivor benefit, and bridge benefit details. The official number still comes from your plan administrator.
