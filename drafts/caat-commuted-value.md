# CAAT Commuted Value: DBplus and DBprime Leaving Options

Status: Publication authorized November 13, 2026; source draft for release. Source review and implementation QA: `docs/caat-guide-qa.md`.

Leaving a CAAT employer starts a pension decision that can unfold over several years. DBplus and DBprime have different benefit formulas and retirement rules. The important questions are which benefits you earned, when membership ends, and whether a transfer is available—not just how large a lump sum might be.

This independent guide is general education, not financial, tax, legal or actuarial advice. CVCalculator is not affiliated with CAAT. Governing plan terms, applicable law and CAAT's official determination control your entitlement.

## What is a CAAT commuted value?

A commuted value (CV) puts a present-day lump sum on future pension benefits. It is not simply your contributions plus your employer's contributions. [FSRA's member guide](https://www.fsrao.ca/consumers/pensions/member-guide-you-terminated-employment-what-commuted-value) explains the general concept and the benefits surrendered when a transfer is completed.

Keep three questions separate: **eligibility** determines whether you can transfer, **valuation** determines the amount, and **tax and locking-in rules** determine where it can go.

## DBplus versus DBprime: identify your benefits first

Both are defined benefit designs within CAAT. DBplus does not become a personal investment account because contributions enter its pension formula.

- **DBprime:** the pension formula uses pensionable service and highest average pensionable earnings.
- **DBplus:** the pension formula uses member and employer contributions, a pension factor and conditional Average Industrial Wage enhancements. Its formula differs from DBprime's.

CAAT's [retirement planning guide](https://www.caatpension.ca/documents/members%28new%29/member-resources/Retirement-planning-guide-en.pdf) explains the designs. Start with the accrued benefits on your statement rather than converting salary or contributions into a guessed CV.

### Mixed benefits and merged plans

Changing employment type can leave you with benefits under both designs. CAAT's [continuing-your-pension booklet](https://www.caatpension.ca/content/dam/caatwebsite/documentprocessed/members%28new%29/member-resources/Inter-design-transfer-booklet-en.pdf) describes a pension with separately calculated DBprime and DBplus portions. Do not apply a DBplus age rule to every part of a mixed pension. If a former plan merged into CAAT, ask which merger provisions govern your benefits too.

## The 24-month membership extension

CAAT's [leaving-your-job guidance](https://www.caatpension.ca/members/leaving-your-job) describes an automatic 24-month extension of membership after contributions stop when employment ends before normal retirement. Returning to a participating employer can mean resuming contributions. Moving to another employer's pension plan is a separate possible transfer route, subject to acceptance and applicable conditions.

CAAT also describes conditional Average Industrial Wage increases during the extension. The extension is not a promise of immediate access to a LIRA. It also means your age when you leave work can differ from your age when membership ends. Ask CAAT to confirm the actual membership-end date, including any return to work, disability or other circumstances affecting it.

## Can you transfer your CAAT commuted value?

For the ordinary post-extension transfer to a locked-in retirement arrangement, CAAT's public guidance identifies these screening rules:

- **DBplus:** be under 50 at the end of the extension.
- **DBprime:** be under 55 at the end of the extension, with earlier retirement eligibility also relevant.

For DBprime, being under 55 alone is insufficient. The [plan text, sections 7.01 and 10.02](https://www.caatpension.ca/about-us/member-policies.html), links ordinary portability to not being entitled to start an early pension. Section 7.01 includes age 50 with 20 years of pensionable service, age 55, or an age-plus-service total of 85. The 20-year boundary includes exactly 20 years; it should not be read as only more than 20.

The plan text provides a six-month election period after membership ends for the ordinary former-member CV route. That window does not override retirement-eligibility restrictions. A transfer to another accepting pension plan has separate provisions; losing the ordinary LIRA route does not establish that every transfer is unavailable.

**Confirm your entitlement:** Ask CAAT which design, service test, age date and election deadline apply to each benefit component. This guide does not determine mixed-benefit, merger or jurisdiction-specific eligibility.

## Eligibility dates and valuation dates do different jobs

Read your package as a sequence of events:

1. **Employment and contributions end:** establish the starting facts; these dates may need clarification.
2. **The membership extension ends:** establishes the ordinary post-extension decision point.
3. **CAAT values the benefit:** its leaving guidance describes calculation at the end of the extension using the rates then applicable.
4. **You make an election and payment follows:** confirm the response date, continuing eligibility, quote validity, and any recalculation or payment-interest treatment separately.

An estimate made before departure is not a promise of the amount payable after the extension. The [DBplus member handbook](https://www.caatpension.ca/content/dam/caatwebsite/documentprocessed/members%28new%29/member-resources/member-handbook-dbplus-gp-d2-en.pdf) also distinguishes the extension from the subsequent election window.

## Retain the pension or make an eligible transfer?

Retaining a pension preserves future income under CAAT's terms. CAAT's [deferred-pension guidance](https://www.caatpension.ca/members/starting-your-deferred-pension) describes lifetime payments and conditional inflation protection. Conditional increases are not a guaranteed fixed annual return. Ask for pension amounts at the start dates actually available to you, including any bridge and survivor benefits.

A completed CV transfer replaces the corresponding plan entitlement with transferred funds. You then take responsibility for investment performance, fees and making retirement assets last. Compare the household income and protections retained or surrendered—not a lump sum against contributions alone. The [FSRA guide](https://www.fsrao.ca/consumers/pensions/member-guide-you-terminated-employment-what-commuted-value) explains these trade-offs without making the decision for you.

## Why can an estimated value change?

A present value depends on the benefits, valuation date and assumptions. With the same future payments and other assumptions unchanged, a higher discount rate generally produces a lower present value. That relationship does not forecast your eventual CAAT settlement.

The general [interest-rate explanation](/how-cia-3500-interest-rates-affect-commuted-value) introduces the concept. CAAT must confirm the calculation basis, applicable rates and benefit adjustments for your case; this guide does not assume every design uses the same assumptions as a third-party calculator.

## Tax and locking-in: two different restrictions

Locked-in money is reserved for retirement under the applicable pension rules; a transfer is not automatically spendable cash. A tax-deferred transfer is also not the same as an unrestricted withdrawal.

For relevant DB-to-RRSP-type or defined-contribution transfers, [Income Tax Regulations section 8517](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._945/section-8517.html) sets a prescribed transfer amount. It does not calculate the pension's CV. [CRA distinguishes these transfers from DB-to-DB transfers](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/registered-plans-administrators/newsletters-technical-manual/no-04-1.html).

Excess paid in cash is taxable and subject to withholding; withholding is not necessarily the final tax bill. Any separate RRSP contribution or deduction depends on available room and the relevant conditions. See our [LIRA transfer-limit explanation](/commuted-value-lira-transfer-limit-explained) and [commuted-value tax guide](/commuted-value-taxes-canada). Ask CAAT to identify tax excess and any contribution refund separately.

### Which jurisdiction applies?

CAAT's [jurisdiction guidance](https://www.caatpension.ca/members/member-resources/jurisdictional-differences) says its general provisions are based on Ontario legislation and describes differences for other jurisdictions. Spouse definitions, consent requirements, small-pension rules and locking-in can differ. Do not substitute your current home province for CAAT's determination of the jurisdiction governing your benefits.

Small-pension payments are a separate route; do not assume ordinary transfer cutoffs describe every possible lump sum. Federal tax rules apply alongside pension rules. CAAT benefits subject to federal pension jurisdiction are not the Federal Public Service Pension Plan and do not inherit that plan's transfer-value deadlines.

## Common misconceptions

- **“I was under the cutoff when I quit, so I can transfer.”** Check the membership-end date and applicable retirement entitlement.
- **“Six months means my quote cannot change.”** Election timing and valuation validity are different questions.
- **“One combined pension amount is enough for any calculator.”** Mixed benefits can have different retirement and indexing features.
- **“A large CV can all go to a LIRA.”** Eligibility, tax limits and locking-in must each be checked.

## Questions to ask CAAT before deciding

- Which DBplus, DBprime, merger and supplementary components have I earned?
- What are my employment-end, last-contribution and membership-end dates?
- Which retirement entitlement affects portability, including the exact service boundary?
- What are my election deadline, age cutoff, valuation date and quote-validity period?
- Can I transfer to my new employer's plan, and how would it credit the transfer?
- Which jurisdiction, spouse-consent, tax and locking-in requirements apply?
- Which amounts are pension value, taxable excess, refunds or separate arrangements?
- What pension income, inflation protection and survivor benefits would I retain or surrender?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a CAAT commuted value?

It is a lump-sum present value of pension benefits, not simply a refund of member and employer contributions. CAAT determines the official amount and whether a transfer is available.

### Can DBplus members transfer before age 50?

Being under 50 when leaving work is not enough. CAAT's ordinary post-extension route requires being under 50 when the extension ends, with applicable eligibility and election conditions still to be satisfied.

### Does every DBprime member under 55 qualify?

No. Early-pension entitlement can exclude the ordinary transfer route. The plan text includes age 50 with 20 years of pensionable service and an age-plus-service total of 85 among the retirement tests.

### Does the six-month election period freeze the value?

No fixed quote is promised by the election period itself. Ask CAAT to identify the valuation date, quote validity, any recalculation and the treatment of interest before payment.

### Can I apply one design's rules to mixed benefits?

Do not assume so. Ask CAAT to identify each component and explain how the designs and any merger provisions interact for your eligibility and benefit amounts.
